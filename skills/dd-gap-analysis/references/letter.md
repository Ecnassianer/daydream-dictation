# Gap Analysis: Letters

Read this file only during a `dd-gap-analysis` run, and only when the document is a letter: anything addressed to a reader or an audience, such as a cover letter, an email, a memo, a recommendation, or an open letter.

## What a good letter has

- **A purpose that fits the kind of letter.** A letter meant to convince or achieve something makes its purpose clear from reading it. A letter meant just to connect doesn't need an objective, and that's fine.
- **A reader the tone fits.** For a letter to one person, the tone, length, and level of detail fit who they are and what they already know. An open letter has a broad or unknown audience, so it fits the readers it most wants to reach.
- **A specific ask**, if the letter wants something: what, by when, and how to respond.
- **Evidence that fits the ask.** Concrete facts, examples, or numbers, and only as many as the case needs.
- **Nothing the reader has to dig for.** Background comes after the point, not before it.
- **A close that matches the open**, with the sign-off, the sender's name, and contact details where the reader needs them.

## Questions

### Who is the reader, and does the letter fit them?

Name the recipient, or for an open letter the audience it most wants to reach, and what the document implies they already know. Flag jargon they won't share, background they don't need, and a tone (too formal, too casual, too long) that works against the purpose.

### Is the purpose clear?

Decide whether the letter is meant to convince or achieve something, or just to connect. If it's meant to achieve something, check that a reader would know what from reading it, and flag a purpose that's buried or missing. If it serves more than one purpose, ask whether it should be split. If it's meant to connect, skip this question.

### Is the ask specific?

If the letter wants the reader to do something, check that it says what, by when, and how. Flag asks that are implied but never stated, and asks buried in the middle. Skip this question if the letter asks for nothing, such as a thank-you note.

### Does every paragraph earn its place?

Flag paragraphs that don't support the purpose or the ask, repeated points, and claims with no supporting fact. Suggest cuts, not just additions; letters get worse as they get longer.

### Is the close complete?

Check the ending: does it restate the ask or next step, and does it have a sign-off, the sender's name, and any contact details or attachments the body promised?
